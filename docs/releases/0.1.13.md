# 0.1.13 — Continuous listening

Server deployed from `streaming-release` on 21 October 2026. The 0.1.13 package is ready; Chrome Web Store submission is waiting for access to the unlocked Mac. The verified preview download and updated product/privacy pages are live on the website. Version 0.1.12 remains the published store release.

- Replaces independent audio uploads with continuous transcription.
- Restores interrupted connections from saved transcript progress, including a cursor inside an audio frame. Unfinished audio stays in a bounded browser buffer.
- Keeps provisional words out of task suggestions, searches and recaps. Recap writes can recover after a lost response without repeating sentences.
- Releases audio inputs immediately on stop and drains the remaining audio before confirming completion.
- Removes the retired upload/decoder implementation, its tests, one-off benchmark runners, FFmpeg, NumPy and unused CLI dependencies. Historical comparison results remain outside the container.

## Boundaries and cost

Audio is mono PCM16 at 16 kHz in frames of at most 200 ms, approximately 115 MB per audio hour before protocol overhead. Acknowledgments count durably committed samples; they are not frame receipts. There is no per-frame encryption, storage write, or language-model request. Recap sequence numbers remain compatible with the existing recap worker.

The browser retains at most 90 seconds of uncommitted audio, retries an outage for at most 60 seconds, and allows at most five reconnections per capture. Catch-up is paced at up to 2x real time. Recovery requires the offscreen page and its in-memory audio buffer to remain alive; a browser crash cannot restore unsaved audio.

Normal final results retain the recognition context. A 30-second gap without a final result drains and replaces the context; a three-hour context limit also permits long meetings. Old input ends and its results drain before the replacement opens. A meeting remains capped at six audio hours. Timeouts report incomplete capture instead of claiming it was saved.

The normal model-rate difference remains approximately $0.09 per audio hour at the checked regional prices. Replayed unfinished audio and per-session rounding can add transcription charges. Traffic, saved-state operations and server capacity remain usage-dependent. This is not a fixed all-inclusive price or a hard account spending cap. No new worker, server, NAT gateway or load balancer is required. The existing server size and standard CPU-credit mode are preserved.

## Production rollout

Three concurrent production streams completed 93.37 seconds of combined input, covering reconnect recovery, recorded human speech, and noise followed by silence. Durable cursors reached the exact input lengths. The reconnect case retained both repetitions of its reference text without adding another. An older client sent audio through the retained legacy endpoint successfully. The connection limit rejected an extra socket before opening another paid transcription stream.

The built offscreen capture also passed a muted synthetic browser check against the production endpoint: two connections, provisional and final text, all three reference sentences exactly once, no participant-facing error, final audio drained, both audio inputs released. No real microphone input or speaker playback was used.

The existing t3.micro remains in standard CPU-credit mode. The streaming container uses a 256 MiB limit and measured 116.1 MiB after these checks, with no restart or out-of-memory event. The existing legacy service stays available for older installed extensions. These short checks establish bounded concurrent operation, not an hours-long endurance or maximum-capacity claim.

Production checks found and fixed a burst limiter that could reject valid buffered audio and transient DynamoDB transaction conflicts between meetings sharing a login. The limiter permits bounded bursts while retaining a sustained 2x real-time limit. Storage retries at most three identical transactions only for transaction conflicts; version and revocation failures still fail immediately.

The server requires `transcribe:StartStreamTranscription` in its own region. The private infrastructure branch adds only this permission, with checks that the resource topology and cost controls remain unchanged.

Compatibility routing is deployed: `/ws/meeting/stream` serves 0.1.13 continuous audio, while the exact `/ws/meeting` route retains the previous service for installed older extensions. Other app requests use the updated service. The previous container was not restarted during migration. Remove the legacy service only after confirming older installations have migrated; no scheduled retirement worker was added.

A real Google Meet capture check and a two-person Zoom browser audio/reconnect/recap check remain required to finish live-call validation. Zoom stays a browser preview; Zoom desktop is unsupported. Local fixtures and synthetic browser/AWS checks do not replace those live-call checks. The downloadable build remains a preview until they are completed.
